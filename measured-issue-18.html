The Election Window · Nov 4 – Today

Rates dropped 28 basis points
the week after the election.
Then gave almost all of it back.

On November 4th — the day after Election Day — the 10-year Treasury closed at 4.90% and the 30-year fixed was 7.17%. That was the number. Democrats took both the House and Senate, fiscal uncertainty eased, and bond markets exhaled. By November 5th the 10-year was at 4.84% and mortgage rates had dipped to their lowest level since September — 7.09% on some surveys.

That window lasted about two weeks.

By November 13th, the MBA's 30-year conforming rate had climbed back to 7.35%. And Freddie Mac's November 19 reading came in at 7.28% — up 17 basis points in a single week, the biggest one-week jump since the September hike. The 10-year is now hovering around 4.97%.

The lesson in that two-week window: rate moves driven by sentiment reverse faster than moves driven by data. The election wasn't a fundamental change to the inflation picture. Oil is still above $95. The Fed is still signaling a December hike. The 10-year doesn't care about which party controls Congress when PCE is running hot.

Non-QM · The Category Most Advisors Are Still Sleeping On

1 in 10 mortgages
is now Non-QM.
Are you writing them?

As of July 2026, Non-QM loans crossed 10% of total rate-lock volume — a record. Conforming loans, which made up 52% of production just a year earlier, are now below 48% of the market. The shift isn't a blip. It's structural.

Three product types are driving it:

  • Bank Statement loans — 30.6% of Non-QM production. Self-employed borrowers who run income through their business and can't show it on a 1040 the way an underwriter wants to see it. These borrowers exist in every market. Most of them have been told no by someone who didn't know the product.
  • DSCR / Investor loans — 33.5% of Non-QM production, up nearly 5 points year-over-year. Debt Service Coverage Ratio loans qualify on the property's income, not the borrower's W-2. If the rent covers the mortgage, the loan works. These are the fastest-growing segment in the channel.
  • Expanded-guideline products — 35.9%. Asset depletion, 1-year tax return, recent credit events, non-warrantable condos. Borrowers that conventional and FHA simply cannot touch — but wholesale can.

Here is the practical reality: in a market where purchase applications are 18% below last year and refi volume is down 54%, the advisors adding new business are the ones expanding their qualified borrower pool. Non-QM is not a niche product. It is a business strategy.

The wholesale channel runs these products at rates and terms retail simply can't match. If you have a referral partner — a realtor, a financial planner, a CPA — who works with business owners or investors, you have a Non-QM pipeline. It just might not know you do.

Market Data · Applications + Housing Starts

Apps are down. Permits
hit a 6-year low. But
starts bounced — here's why.

The October housing data tells a split story that's worth understanding before you have your next conversation with a buyer who's on the fence.

Housing starts for October rose 9.9% month-over-month to a 1.309 million annualized rate — the headline number most outlets ran with. But look one line down: building permits fell 7.4% and are now at their lowest level since May 2020. Single-family permits dropped 8.7% in a single month. Builders are completing the pipeline they already have. They are not starting new one.

That distinction matters. Permits are the leading indicator. When permit issuance drops to a 6-year low, the message from builders is: at 7.4% rates, with affordability this stretched, we are not betting on demand coming back in the next 12–18 months. That is not a vote of confidence in the near-term market.

Meanwhile, new-home mortgage applications fell 13.6% year-over-year in October — the fourth straight annual decline. The average loan size on a new-home purchase dropped to $368,934 from $374,724 the prior month. Buyers are coming in lower, taking smaller homes, or walking away entirely.

The existing-home picture is equally telling. Median sale prices have fallen year-over-year for four straight weeks — the first time that's happened since 2023. 20.7% of active listings have had a price reduction, up from 19.6% the week prior. There are over half a million more sellers than buyers nationally. Pending sales are running 15% below last year. The gap between what sellers want and what buyers can afford — at 7.28% rates — is not closing.

What This Means for Buyers in Your Pipeline
Redfin calls this the strongest buyer's market since 2013. Months of supply are at 4.8. Negotiating power has shifted. The buyers who act now — before December's probable hike — are negotiating on price and catching rates before they move higher. That is a conversation worth having with anyone who has been sitting on the sidelines.
Operations · AI · Speed to Lead

Grok, AI tools, and
the efficiency gap that's
separating advisors right now.

At Westlake Village, the conversation about AI tools wasn't theoretical. The advisors and account managers in that room who are using AI operationally — not for content, for actual workflow — are running leaner and moving faster than advisors who aren't. The gap is widening.

The specific application that keeps coming up: speed to lead. Studies across the mortgage industry consistently show that the first advisor to respond to an inquiry wins the deal at a disproportionate rate — not the best rate sheet, not the most experienced loan officer. The first one to respond. AI changes the math here fundamentally. An automated first-touch that asks the right qualifying questions, confirms availability, and schedules a setting call — triggered the moment a lead comes in — is not science fiction. It is table stakes in 2026.

Grok and similar AI tools are being used right now for scenario building, objection prep, pre-qual analysis, and market commentary. For account managers specifically, the opportunity is around the volume of repetitive communication that eats the day — status updates, rate change notifications, pipeline follow-ups. Templated, well-written, personalized at scale. That is what's available today.

The advisors using these tools aren't replacing the relationship. They're protecting the time that lets the relationship exist. The goal is simple: make the human conversation better by automating everything that doesn't need to be human.

"Speed to lead isn't about being aggressive. It's about being available. The borrower who submits a form at 7:14pm on a Tuesday and hears back in four minutes — that advisor wins the conversation before anyone else even wakes up."

The setting call matters. Getting a prospect on the phone for 15 minutes — before they've talked to three other advisors — is the whole game. AI gets you to that call faster. The relationship closes it.

The Agent Relationship

What agents actually want
from their mortgage advisor
in a market like this.

Right now, most agents are having conversations with buyers who are frustrated, confused, or just waiting. Waiting for rates to drop. Waiting for prices to fall more. Waiting for something to change. The agents who are still closing deals in this environment are the ones with advisors who arm them with language — specific, data-backed, honest language — that moves a conversation forward instead of parking it.

Three things agents in this market want from their mortgage advisor:

  • A weekly rate update they can actually use in conversation — not just a number, but context. "Rates are 7.28% this week — here's what moved them and here's what December looks like" is a sentence an agent can use with a client on a showing.
  • A fast pre-approval process that doesn't embarrass them — in a buyer's market, agents are using pre-approval speed as a competitive advantage. If your process is 48 hours while another advisor is doing same-day, you are costing your agents deals.
  • Scenario clarity on Non-QM and non-standard borrowers — agents are increasingly running into self-employed buyers, investors, and borrowers with complicated income. The advisor who can say "I have a product for that" is the one who gets called first on the next deal.

The relationship economy in real estate hasn't changed. What has changed is that the advisors who show up with information — real market data, real product knowledge, real availability — are the ones agents are calling. The ones who show up with rate sheets and a smile are getting filtered out.
