Thursday's bond rally — the biggest since March 2023 — and where we closed today

To understand this week you need to understand what happened in a single Thursday trading session — and why mortgage rates didn't fall as much as the bond market did.

The 10-year Treasury yield opened Thursday morning near 5.13% — right where it had been hovering all week after the 10-year peaked at 5.3645% on October 7, its highest level since April 2002. Then Meta's earnings sent the stock lower by 7%, triggering a broad AI and tech selloff. Investors moved quickly into the perceived safety of government bonds. The result: the 10-year fell nearly 20 basis points in a single session — its largest one-day drop since March 10, 2023. The 10-year closed Thursday at 4.96% — the first close below 5% since September 22.

📉 Thursday's Bond Market Move — The Numbers

10-year Treasury yield: Fell from 5.13% to 4.96% — a drop of 17 basis points in one session. Largest single-day decline since March 2023.

30-year Treasury yield: Fell more than 8 basis points to 5.351% — the longer bond is more sensitive to geopolitical and inflation expectations.

2-year Treasury yield: Fell more than 9 basis points to 4.731% — even the short end moved, unusual in a pure flight-to-safety trade.

MBS — UMBS 30YR 6.0: Hit 99.46 on Friday — their best level in a month. When MBS prices rise, lenders can offer lower rates. This is the mechanism that translates bond market moves into your actual mortgage quote.

What drove it: Not good inflation data. Not a breakthrough on Iran or the Strait of Hormuz. A tech stock rout — Meta down 7%, broader AI selloff, investors seeking cover. The bond market rewarded fear, not fundamentals. That is exactly why this move may not hold.

Today, Friday October 23, the 10-year is at 4.989% — holding just under 5% as the session closes. The modest uptick from Thursday's close reflects some stabilization in equities. Mortgage News Daily's real-time rate index shows the 30-year at 7.33% today, up 3 basis points from Thursday's intraday low — still the best retail rate in about a month.

⚠️ Why This Rally May Be Short-Lived

The bond market moved on fear, not on a fundamental improvement in the inflation picture. The 10-year peaked at 5.3645% on October 7 — its highest since 2002 — as oil pushed above $100 per barrel and the FOMC's October hike probability climbed above 70%. None of those underlying forces have changed. Iran negotiations remain inconclusive. Oil is still elevated. The Fed meets Tuesday and is widely expected to hike again.

As 24/7 Wall Street put it plainly this morning: "If the 10-year closes back above 5.13% on Treasury's par curve, the whole fear rally will have reversed." That is the level to watch. If next week's FOMC signals a December hike is still on the table — or if PCE Wednesday comes in hot — the 10-year moves back above 5% fast and the brief rate relief evaporates. The window between a rate improvement and a reversal in this environment is measured in hours, not weeks.

Next week — FOMC Tuesday, PCE Wednesday. Everything hinges on these two.

The two most consequential market events of the month happen in the next five days. Every borrower, every real estate agent, and every lender in America should have Tuesday and Wednesday circled.

📅 The Week Ahead · October 27–31, 2026

Tuesday–Wednesday Oct 27–28 — FOMC Meeting · Decision Wednesday 2:00pm ET. The market is pricing approximately a 65–70% probability of another 25-basis-point hike — which would bring the fed funds rate to 4.00–4.25%. This hike has been in the price for weeks. What the market doesn't know yet is what Warsh says in his Wednesday press conference about December. If he signals December is also live, the 10-year climbs back through 5% immediately and the week's bond rally reverses. If he signals a pause is possible, rates could hold near current levels or improve further. Listen to every word of his press conference — not just the rate decision itself.

Wednesday Oct 29 — PCE Inflation (September) · 8:30am ET. Released the same morning as the FOMC decision day — creating one of the most data-dense Wednesdays of the year. September PCE is the last major inflation reading before the Fed decides. Core PCE has been running above 3% all year. If September shows any deceleration — even a move toward 2.8% — it strengthens the case for a December pause and bond markets rally further. A hot print at or above 3.3% and the hike narrative accelerates into year-end. Set an alarm for 8:30am Wednesday. This number and the Fed decision together will define the rate environment through Thanksgiving.

Friday Oct 31 — Employment Cost Index (Q3). A measure of wage inflation — one of the stickiest and most Fed-watched inflation inputs. A reading above 1.1% quarter-over-quarter keeps the hike narrative intact. A softer reading removes one more argument for continued tightening.

Daily — WTI crude oil and Strait of Hormuz. Still the override variable. Oil fell sharply on Thursday as part of the broader market move, but remains elevated. Any credible signal of a Hormuz agreement sends oil toward $80, pulls inflation expectations down, and is worth 20–30 basis points of rate improvement within days — independent of everything the Fed says. Watch oil every morning before anything else.

What the rate actually is — retail vs. wholesale, today

The Freddie Mac 7.40% headline has been the dominant narrative in mortgage rate coverage for two straight weeks. It is accurate as a weekly average across all lenders and borrower profiles. It is not the rate a well-qualified borrower working with a wholesale advisor is actually seeing.

The Actual Rate Conversation This Week

Freddie Mac's PMMS at 7.40% captures applications from across the entire lender landscape — retail banks, credit unions, online lenders, broker-submitted applications. The average includes less competitive lenders and a wide range of borrower profiles. It is the right number for a headline. It is not the right number for a decision.

Today, Friday October 23, the Mortgage News Daily benchmark sits at 7.33% — the best level in a month. Through the wholesale channel, where lenders compete for broker business rather than pricing for a retail margin, a well-qualified borrower — 740+ FICO, 20% down, primary residence, conventional loan — is looking at rates in the 6.85–6.95% range right now. That is the conversation that doesn't make it into the headline. A year ago that same borrower was at 6.00–6.15% through the same channel. The gap is real. The decision to act now versus wait for a number that may or may not arrive is a personal one — but it should be made with accurate information.

If Thursday's bond rally holds through next week's FOMC — if Warsh signals a December pause — the 30-year retail rate could pull back toward 7.10–7.15% and the wholesale rate approaches 6.65–6.75%. That is a meaningful improvement in monthly payment terms. A $400,000 loan at 6.75% runs approximately $2,594/month in principal and interest. At 7.40% it's $2,764/month. The gap is $170/month — $2,040 per year. That is real money, and it is the range of outcomes next week's data can deliver.
