Rates this week — the highest level since August 2025

The Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey released Thursday July 16 showed the 30-year fixed at 6.55% — up 6 basis points from 6.49% the prior week and the highest reading since August 2025. The 15-year fixed came in at 5.93%, up sharply from 5.82% the week before. The MBA's own rate index for the week ending July 17 showed the 30-year conforming rate reaching 6.69% — its highest level since last August.

The table tells a nuanced story. We are better than a year ago by 20 basis points — meaningful, but not dramatic. We are worse than where rates were in late February before the Iran war, when the 30-year was near 6.00%. And we are at a local high point for 2026, having hit 6.00% in February before the war sent us to 6.55% today.

The year-over-year improvement is real but fragile. Every time the Strait of Hormuz stabilizes, rates improve toward 6.40%. Every time the ceasefire frays, rates push back toward 6.55–6.65%. That band — roughly 6.40% to 6.65% — has been the operating range for mortgage rates throughout the summer of 2026. The wholesale channel runs 0.375–0.50% below that range. That is where the actual opportunity is for well-prepared borrowers.

The 6% week-over-week jump in purchase applications looks strong but needs context. The prior week — ending July 10 — was depressed by the post-July 4 holiday hangover and the ceasefire collapse shock. Purchase applications had dropped 7% that week and dipped 2% below year-ago levels — the first negative year-over-year reading in months. The bounce back this week essentially restored the status quo rather than representing a genuine acceleration.

📊 Purchase Application Trend — Last Four Weeks · MBA Data

Week ending June 26: Purchase index up 1% week over week, up 3% year over year. Holiday adjustment applied.

Week ending July 3: Purchase index down 1% week over week, up 5% year over year. Holiday-shortened week.

Week ending July 10: Purchase index down 7% week over week, down 2% year over year. First negative YoY in months — post-holiday and ceasefire shock combined.

Week ending July 17: Purchase index up 6% week over week, up 0.2% year over year. Recovery bounce — back just barely above last year's pace despite rates at highest level since August 2025.

The honest read: purchase demand is remarkably resilient given where rates are. The MBA's Mike Fratantoni put it directly this week: "Purchase volume increased modestly for the week. Growing home inventory in many markets is supporting more purchase activity." Buyers aren't capitulating. They're adjusting. More inventory is giving them more choices, and the year-over-year comparison — even barely positive at 0.2% — is meaningful when rates are at their highest level in eleven months.

📈 The Year-Over-Year Purchase Story — In Full Context

Through the first half of 2026, purchase applications ran 3–8% ahead of 2025 pace in most weeks. The brief dip to -2% the week of July 10 was an outlier — the combined effect of holiday timing and a geopolitical shock — not a trend reversal. This week's 0.2% positive reading suggests the underlying demand is intact even at 6.55%.

The MBA's own president, Bob Broeksmit, has said homebuyers in 2026 appear to be "resigned to higher-for-longer rates" — not waiting for a breakthrough, but making decisions with the rates available. That behavioral shift is the single most important development in the purchase market this year. Buyers who stopped waiting for 5% and started buying at 6.4% in the wholesale channel are already in homes. The ones still waiting are still waiting.

On the refinance side: applications were down 2% week over week as rising rates reduced the incentive, but up 7% year over year. FHA and VA borrowers continue to drive the refi activity — these are 2023–2024 borrowers who took on 7%+ loans and are finding meaningful savings even in the current rate environment through the wholesale channel.

The consensus is tight and the message is clear: nobody expects rates to fall meaningfully before the end of 2026. The range across all major forecasters for Q4 2026 is 6.3–6.5%. For Q1 2027, the most optimistic scenario — Wells Fargo and Fannie Mae — has the 30-year around 6.2–6.3%. The MBA holds steady at 6.5% through all of 2027.

⚠️ Why the Forecasts Are Not as Optimistic as They Were in January

Fannie Mae's January 2026 forecast had the 30-year reaching 5.7% by Q4 2026. Today that same institution forecasts 6.4%. The gap between those two projections — nearly 70 basis points — is almost entirely attributable to the Iran war, the Strait of Hormuz closure, and the energy-driven inflation surge that followed. Oil is the variable that no forecast model had sized correctly entering 2026.

The MBA economists summed it up plainly in their latest commentary: "We expect CPI inflation will peak above 4% and remain elevated for the next year or so. With this higher inflation forecast, Treasury yields and mortgage rates will stay higher for longer." That's the forecasting community's honest acknowledgment that the Iran shock changed the rate trajectory in ways that can't be wished away.

📉 The One Scenario That Changes Everything

Every major forecast carries an implicit assumption: the Iran conflict continues without full resolution through year-end. If that assumption breaks — if a durable peace deal is signed and oil falls back toward $65–70 per barrel — all of these forecasts move lower simultaneously. A sustained oil price at $68 with a credible Strait reopening is worth approximately 30–40 basis points of mortgage rate improvement within 60 days, based on the market's own demonstrated behavior when the June Versailles deal was announced.

That scenario would put the 30-year at 6.10–6.20% retail — and closer to 5.70–5.80% through the wholesale channel — before Q4 2026. No forecaster is publishing that as a base case. But it's the scenario the market would price in within hours of confirmation. Being positioned before that announcement is what separates prepared borrowers from reactive ones.

Anthony's Take — What These Forecasts Actually Mean For You

The consensus tells you the 30-year stays in the 6.3–6.5% range through Q1 2027 in the base case. Through the wholesale channel, that's roughly 5.85–6.10% on your actual loan. That is the real number for a well-qualified borrower working with a broker who has wholesale access — not the 6.55% retail headline.

The question isn't whether rates are good right now in absolute terms. The question is whether the rate you can access today — and the home you can buy today — is a better position than where you'll be in Q1 2027 after another six months of rent payments, rising prices in your target markets, and continued supply constraints. For most buyers who are qualified and have found the right property, the math already answers that question. The hard part is being willing to run it.
