The September 10 reading — 6.76% — was the last week the 30-year was clearly below 7% on the Freddie Mac survey. Rates crossed 7% on the September 24 reading, the first time above that threshold since January 2025. The move from 6.71% on September 3 to 7.03% on September 24 is 32 basis points in three weeks — driven by the 10-year Treasury climbing to a 19-year high of 5.18% and oil pushing back above $94 per barrel. Today the 10-year is at 5.209%, and real-time rate data from Optimal Blue sits at 7.23% with Bankrate at 7.36%. The Freddie Mac weekly survey is always a few days behind the live market.

For a well-qualified borrower in the wholesale channel today, the actual rate conversation is in the 6.75–7.00% range — the 0.375–0.50% wholesale advantage is more meaningful right now than at any point this year. The 15-year moved from 6.04% on September 3 to 6.42% on September 24. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.30%. The year-over-year gap is now 73–100 basis points depending on the source and date of comparison.

⚠️ What's Driving This — And What Comes Next

Three forces in overlap: the Fed hike — unanimous, first in three years, with 16 of 19 FOMC members projecting at least one more before year-end. The 10-year Treasury at 5.18–5.21% — reflecting persistent inflation, fiscal concerns, and higher-for-longer policy expectations. And oil back above $94 per barrel, keeping energy-driven inflation elevated and removing any cover for the Fed to pause.

The one potential relief valve: signs have emerged that U.S. and Iranian negotiators may be exploring a phased agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which briefly pulled the 10-year off its highs Friday. Markets are currently pricing a 70% probability of another 25-basis-point hike at the October 28 meeting. Tuesday's PCE release and Friday's jobs report will move that probability more than anything else this week.

Rates have been above 7% for over a week — and people are still buying houses

Three purchase clients this week. I want to sit with that for a moment before diving into the data, because it says something that a rate chart doesn't: motivated buyers with real reasons to move are not waiting for a number on a screen to change. Life doesn't pause for rate cycles. Families grow. Jobs move. Estates settle. Downsizing decisions get made on a Tuesday morning when someone sees a price cut they've been watching for months.

Prices are not declining nationally — the median sale price of $395,000 in August was up 2.1% year-over-year. What is changing is negotiating leverage. With 1.42 million active listings — up 42.7% from three years ago — buyers are finding more choices, more days to decide, and more willingness from sellers to cover closing costs or offer rate buydowns. The buyers who have been pre-approved and waiting for the market to "improve" are discovering that the best leverage point isn't a lower rate. It's a motivated seller in a market with more inventory than buyers.

Be available when others aren't — the pre-approval that came before 8am

One of the three purchases this week came in a way I want to share because it's a simple reminder of something that gets forgotten in a busy market.

A past client — someone I did a cash-out refinance for last year — is downsizing. He's been watching a specific area, tracking prices. Earlier this week, before 8am, he saw a price cut hit on a house in Lincolnton. Ten thousand dollars off. He called me five minutes later. By the time he was done with his coffee, he had a pre-approval letter in his inbox and an agent on the phone.

On Being Available

That deal didn't happen because I had the best rate or the most sophisticated CRM. It happened because I picked up the phone before 8am. The window between a buyer seeing a price cut and making an offer can be hours in a market with real competition. A client who has to wait until business hours to reach their lender is a client who might lose the deal — or worse, find another lender who was available.

Availability is a competitive advantage that most people in this business underestimate. It doesn't mean being on call 24 hours a day without boundaries. It means that your best clients — the ones who trust you, who you've served well, who refer you business — know that when something real shows up, you'll be there. That reputation is built one answered call at a time. You can't manufacture it. You can only earn it.

This week is one of the most data-heavy of 2026 — here's every release that matters
📅 This Week Is One of the Most Important of the Year — Sept 29 – Oct 3

Monday Sept 29 — JOLTS (Job Openings, August) · 10:00am ET. The first major data point of the week. Job openings peaked above 12 million in 2022 and have been steadily declining. A reading below 7.5 million signals real labor market softening — bond-market friendly. This sets the tone heading into a heavy-data Tuesday.

Tuesday Sept 30 — PCE Inflation (August) · 8:30am ET. This is the big one. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge — the exact number Warsh cited when he hiked on September 16. Core PCE has been running above 3% all year. If August shows even modest moderation toward 2.8–3.0%, the market will immediately reprice the probability of an October hike lower. Rates could improve 15–25 basis points within hours of this release. A hot print at 3.3%+ cements October as a live hike meeting. Set an alarm. This number matters more for your borrowers than anything else this week.

Tuesday Sept 30 — Q2 GDP Third Estimate · 8:30am ET. Released simultaneously with PCE. The third and final read on Q2 economic growth. Consensus is around 2.1% annualized. A downward revision toward 1.5% or below introduces recession risk into the conversation and is paradoxically favorable for rates — the Fed has less room to keep hiking if the economy is slowing.

Wednesday Oct 1 — ISM Manufacturing PMI · 10:00am ET. A reading above 50 = expansion; below 50 = contraction. Manufacturing has been in contraction territory for much of 2026. A second consecutive sub-48 reading reinforces the slowdown narrative and softens rate hike expectations going into October 28.

Friday Oct 2 — Jobs Report (September) · 8:30am ET. The single most market-moving release of the month. July printed -23,000 — a genuine job loss. August is expected to bounce back toward 80,000–100,000. If September comes in soft again, the October hike discussion becomes a hold discussion almost immediately. If it prints above 150,000, Warsh hikes October 28 and the 30-year pushes toward 7.50%. This number will define the rate environment through year-end more than any single data point remaining.

Tuesday Oct 14 — CPI (September) · 8:30am ET. The last major inflation read before the October 28–29 FOMC meeting. Combined with PCE on Tuesday and the jobs report Friday, this completes the picture. October CPI + September Jobs together will either confirm a second hike or force a pause.

Tuesday–Wednesday Oct 28–29 — FOMC Meeting. The rate decision the entire market is watching. Currently priced at 70% probability of another 25-basis-point hike. That probability will move significantly based on the data released between now and then — starting this Tuesday with PCE.
